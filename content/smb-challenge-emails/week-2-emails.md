## Day 8: Decoding Recurring Revenue Models

**Subject: The $100K/month that almost nobody counts**

[First Name],

Two buyers looked at the same HVAC company.

Buyer 1 saw: $5M revenue, $700K EBITDA
Buyer 2 saw: $5M revenue, $700K EBITDA + $1.2M in hidden recurring revenue

Guess who got the deal for $2M less?

Here's what Buyer 1 missed:

The company had 400 maintenance contracts generating $100K/month. The seller counted this as "service revenue" mixed with repairs.

But maintenance contracts aren't service calls. They're SUBSCRIPTION revenue.

**Here's the recurring revenue decoder ring:**

**MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)**
- Maintenance agreements
- Monitoring services  
- Software subscriptions
- Retainer contracts

**ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)**
- Seasonal contracts
- Annual inspections
- Warranty programs
- Service packages

**Contracted Revenue**
- Multi-year agreements
- Government contracts
- Master service agreements
- Blanket purchase orders

Look at this moving company example:

- Base info: $3.4M revenue, $526K EBITDA
- Hidden gem: 30% from corporate contracts
- Real story: Office moving contracts renew annually
- True value: Predictable revenue worth 2X more

**THE VALUATION IMPACT:**

One-time revenue = 2-4X multiple
Recurring revenue = 5-8X multiple
Contracted revenue = 6-10X multiple

That HVAC company?
- Service calls: $3.8M × 3X = $11.4M
- Maintenance contracts: $1.2M × 7X = $8.4M
- Total real value: $19.8M (not $15M asking)

**QUESTIONS THAT REVEAL HIDDEN RECURRING REVENUE:**

1. "What percentage of revenue comes from repeat customers?"
2. "Do you have any service agreements or contracts?"
3. "How many customers have you served for 3+ years?"
4. "What's your customer retention rate?"
5. "Show me your revenue by customer report"

**RED FLAGS:**
- "It's all project-based"
- "Customers call when they need us"
- Can't produce customer list

**GREEN FLAGS:**
- Maintenance agreement files
- Auto-renewal contracts
- Multi-year master agreements
- 80%+ customer retention

**ADVANCED MOVE:** 

Ask for the "aged receivables report." 

Why? Recurring revenue customers pay like clockwork. If you see the same names month after month, you've found hidden recurring revenue.

## Day 9: Understanding Specialized Certifications

**Subject: The $50K piece of paper worth $5 million**

[First Name],

True story from last month:

Two environmental testing companies for sale:
- Company A: $1.8M revenue, asking $1.5M
- Company B: $1.8M revenue, asking $2.25M

Same revenue. 50% price difference.

The difference? Company B had something called "NELAP accreditation."

That certification took 3 years and $200K to obtain. But it created an impenetrable moat.

**Here's your certification decoder guide:**

**GOVERNMENT CERTIFICATIONS**
- **8(a)**: Minority-owned, sole-source contracts up to $4M
- **HUBZone**: 10% price advantage on bids
- **SDVOSB**: Veteran set-asides worth billions
- **WBE/DBE**: Women/disadvantaged required percentages

**QUALITY CERTIFICATIONS**
- **ISO 9001**: Basic quality management
- **AS9100**: Aerospace (takes 18+ months)
- **ISO 13485**: Medical devices
- **ISO/IEC 17025**: Testing lab accreditation

**INDUSTRY SPECIFIC**
- **SQF**: Food safety (Level 3 = gold standard)
- **EPA RRP**: Lead renovation (required by law)
- **NELAP**: Environmental testing
- **FDA registration**: Pharmaceutical/medical

**THE MOAT CALCULATION:**

Certification value = (Time to obtain × Cost to obtain × Contracts it unlocks)

That NELAP certification?
- 3 years to obtain
- $200K in costs
- Unlocks state contracts
- Competitors can't bid without it
= Massive pricing power

**QUESTIONS TO ASK:**

1. "What certifications do you hold?"
2. "How long did each take to obtain?"
3. "What percentage of revenue requires these certs?"
4. "When do they expire?"
5. "Are they transferable to a new owner?"

**WARNING:** Some certifications die with the seller (personal licenses). Others transfer with the business (company certifications).

**WHERE CERTIFICATIONS = GOLDMINES:**

- Environmental services
- Food/beverage production
- Medical device manufacturing
- Government contracting
- Aerospace components
- Testing laboratories
- Construction specialties

**INSIDER SECRET:** 

Search "[industry] + required certifications" to find the barriers.

Then search "[certification name] + directory" to find every certified company.

Short list + aging owners = acquisition targets.

**REAL EXAMPLE:**

Student found asbestos removal company:
- Revenue: $2.5M
- EBITDA: $600K
- Certifications: EPA RRP, state licenses in 5 states
- Time to replicate: 2+ years
- Result: Bought for $1.8M, competitor offered $3M one year later

Tomorrow: Government contract analysis (or how to value guaranteed revenue).

**ACTION STEP:** Pick an industry. Find 3 required certifications. Calculate the time/cost to obtain them. That's your moat value.

## Day 10: Government Contract Analysis

**Subject: How to read a government contract (and find $2M in hidden value)**

[First Name],

Last year, a buyer walked away from an IT staffing firm because "the main contract expires next year."

Six months later, another buyer bought it for asking price.

The difference? The second buyer knew how to read government contracts.

That "expiring" contract had five option years. It wasn't expiring - it was barely getting started.

**Here's your government contract cheat sheet:**

**CONTRACT TYPES THAT PRINT MONEY:**

**IDIQ (Indefinite Delivery/Indefinite Quantity)**
- No guaranteed minimum
- But sets ceiling (often $10M+)
- Task orders as needed
- Golden ticket for 5-10 years

**BPA (Blanket Purchase Agreement)**
- Pre-negotiated pricing
- Simplified ordering
- Cuts procurement time 90%
- Sticky as superglue

**GSA Schedules**
- Pre-approved vendor list
- Government Amazon
- 20-year contracts
- Auto-renewal common

**GWAC (Government-Wide Acquisition Contract)**
- Any agency can use
- Massive ceilings ($50B+)
- 10+ year terms
- License to print money

**THE OPTION YEAR SECRET:**

Base contract: 1 year
Option years: 4 years
Total contract: 5 years

But here's the kicker - option years are almost ALWAYS exercised if performance is good.

**HOW TO VALUE GOVERNMENT CONTRACTS:**

1. **Current revenue** × **Remaining years** = **Base value**
2. **Add 80% probability** for option years
3. **Add 60% probability** for recompete win
4. **Subtract 20%** for transition risk

**EXAMPLE:**
$2M/year contract
2 base years left
3 option years available

Calculation:
- Base: $2M × 2 = $4M (100%)
- Options: $2M × 3 × 80% = $4.8M  
- Total value: $8.8M
- Risk adjustment: $8.8M × 80% = $7M

**RED FLAGS IN GOVERNMENT CONTRACTS:**

❌ Single award with recompete soon
❌ Protest history
❌ Poor past performance ratings
❌ Set-aside changing (8a graduating)

**GREEN FLAGS:**

✅ Multiple award IDIQ
✅ Sole source justification
✅ "Exceptional" CPARS ratings
✅ Bridge contracts (shows dependency)
✅ Ceiling increases (shows demand)

**ADVANCED DUE DILIGENCE:**

1. **Check SAM.gov** for active registrations
2. **Search USASpending.gov** for payment history
3. **Review CPARS** for performance ratings
4. **Check GAO.gov** for bid protests
5. **Search FBO.gov** for recompete notices

Tomorrow: Manufacturing margin mastery (why 17% margins are actually 37%).

**ACTION STEP:** Go to USASpending.gov. Search a government contractor in your area. See their actual contract values and payment history. Mind-blowing transparency.

- Myles

P.S. One contractor I know has "won" the same contract for 20 years straight. Five different recompetes. Why? The switching costs are massive and performance was perfect.

## Day 11: Manufacturing Margin Mastery

**Subject: The $500K in "missing" profit hiding in plain sight**

[First Name],

A food manufacturer showed me their P&L:
- Revenue: $5.3M
- Gross margin: 17%
- EBITDA: $925K

I showed them their REAL numbers:
- Revenue: $5.3M  
- True gross margin: 37%
- Hidden EBITDA: $400K
- Real EBITDA: $1.35M

The seller's jaw dropped. He'd been undervaluing his business by $2M.

Here's what most people miss in manufacturing:

**THE CAPACITY UTILIZATION TRAP**

Running at 50% capacity doesn't mean 50% profitable.

Your fixed costs stay the same:
- Rent: Same
- Equipment leases: Same
- Base staff: Same
- Insurance: Same
- Certifications: Same

**THE REAL MARGIN MATH:**

At 50% capacity:
- Fixed costs: $2M
- Variable costs: $1.65M  
- Revenue: $5.3M
- Margin: 17%

At 75% capacity (50% more revenue):
- Fixed costs: $2M (unchanged!)
- Variable costs: $2.48M
- Revenue: $7.95M
- Margin: 37%

That's $1.5M in additional EBITDA with ZERO new fixed costs.

**THE CUSTOMER CONCENTRATION GOLDMINE:**

Buyer sees: "45% customer concentration - RISK!"
I see: "55% capacity for new customers - OPPORTUNITY!"

If they can handle one big customer, they can handle two.

**EQUIPMENT VALUE REALITY CHECK:**

Book value: What the depreciation schedule says
Replacement cost: What it costs to buy today
Liquidation value: What a dealer pays
**Strategic value: What it's worth to YOU**

That food manufacturer had $500K in "book value" equipment.
Replacement cost? $2.1M.
Time to source and install? 18 months.

**QUESTIONS THAT REVEAL HIDDEN VALUE:**

1. "What's your current capacity utilization?"
2. "What's your theoretical max capacity?"
3. "When did you last raise prices?"
4. "What's the lead time for your equipment?"
5. "Show me variable vs fixed cost breakdown"

**THE PRICING POWER TEST:**

Ask: "When did you last lose a customer over price?"

If they can't remember, they're underpriced by 20%+.

**ADVANCED ANALYSIS:**

Look at their shipping costs.

High shipping = customers far away = limited local competition = pricing power

That food manufacturer? Shipping to 6 states. Why? Only certified facility in 500 miles.

**ACTION STEP:** Find a manufacturing listing. Calculate the EBITDA impact of going from current to 85% capacity. I bet it doubles.

- Myles
